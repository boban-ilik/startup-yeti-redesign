# What Is a GTM Engineer? A Founder's Hiring Guide

At a 5 to 50 person B2B SaaS company, the default next revenue hire used to be another SDR. That default is wobbling. In late October 2026, SaaStr argued that agents have absorbed much of SDR work, while reporting the same day that Databricks kept its SDR headcount roughly flat. And nearly every article ranking for "GTM engineer" comes from a company with a stake in the answer: Clay, Apollo and ZoomInfo sell the tools, Norwest is a VC firm.

A GTM engineer builds the systems a revenue team runs on: data enrichment, account and lead scoring, routing, and automated outreach, usually with tools such as Clay, n8n, a CRM and AI agents. As of October 2026, open GTM engineer postings at Clay and Vercel list base pay from $140,000 to $295,000, depending on the role and city. Our rule: hire one once you have a message that already gets replies and enough volume that manual research and routing has become the bottleneck.

## What a GTM engineer actually does

Clay describes the work in three rungs: a clean, deduplicated CRM (data foundation), scores that predict who will buy (data modeling), and outreach, meeting notes and churn or expansion alerts built on them (data activation). Its own warning: ["most companies stumble on the first rung."](https://www.clay.com/blog/gtm-engineering)

Clay's examples: inbound sign-ups get scored and routed to the right rep, reps get a Slack digest when a target account shows a buying signal, a script moves firmographics from call transcripts into the CRM. Its [GTM Engineer, Marketing posting](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/claylabs/9f8067d1-61cf-42cd-aad7-0ac54de8b345) lists the same work: scoring, inbound enrichment and routing, speed-to-lead triggers and SDR handoff logic.

Bloomberry's analysis of GTM engineer postings found Clay the most common tool, then HubSpot (52% of postings), Outreach (49%), Salesforce (45%), Zapier (39%) and n8n (28%). SQL and Python each appeared in 38%. Settle your CRM first, since everything else writes to it; see [HubSpot alternatives and what switching costs](/marketing/hubspot-alternatives).

At the top end it is plain software engineering. Vercel's [GTM Engineer posting](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/vercel/jobs/6225825004) hires a software engineer to build "AI apps and workflows that drive revenue."

## Where the title came from

Clay says it coined the role in 2023. Its August 2025 Series C announcement described the role as created two years earlier and counted over 280 GTM Engineer positions posted. Bloomberry's postings data fit that timeline, with hardly any GTM engineering jobs through 2023, a few in 2024 and a jump in 2025. Its headline 205% growth figure counts GTM engineer and RevOps postings together, not GTM engineers alone.

So the title's loudest promoter is the company that sells the core tool. That does not make the role fake, but judge the hire on your bottleneck, not the buzz.

## GTM engineer vs RevOps vs SDR

| | GTM engineer | RevOps | SDR |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main job | Build systems that find, score, route and contact buyers | Keep CRM process, reporting and forecasts accurate | Research accounts, book first meetings for AEs |
| What they own | Enrichment pipelines, scoring, routing, automated plays, agents | CRM configuration, process governance, forecast | Meetings booked, early pipeline |
| Where AI is changing it (October 2026) | Vercel built a GTM engineering team; an agent cut its SDR function from 10 people to about 1.25 (SaaStr) | SaaStr says agents absorbed big parts of RevOps work | SaaStr calls the human SDR job largely gone; Databricks kept headcount flat |
| Hire this first when (our rule) | The message works and manual research and routing is the bottleneck | Forecasting, CRM hygiene and process are what break | You still need human conversations to find the message, or want future AEs |

Vendors frame the split as build versus maintain. ZoomInfo puts it as "RevOps improves the output. GTM engineers build the machine." Independent data muddies that. Bloomberry found that [9 out of 10 responsibilities in GTM engineer postings also appear in RevOps engineer postings](https://bloomberry.com/blog/i-analyzed-1000-gtm-engineering-jobs-here-is-what-i-learned/) and called the jobs "essentially the same." The difference it did find was emphasis: RevOps postings call out forecast accuracy, GTM engineer postings lean toward outbound and prospecting.

Our reconciliation: at 5 to 50 people this is often the same seat, so pick by bottleneck. Need new pipeline systems built? Hire the GTM engineer profile. Forecasts wrong and the CRM a mess? Hire RevOps.

Two neighbors get confused with this role. Growth engineer postings at Lovable and Notion (October 2026) describe in-product work such as activation and monetization flows, not revenue-team systems. A [forward deployed engineer](/team-management/forward-deployed-engineer) is customer-facing, working inside client accounts rather than on your own pipeline.

## What a GTM engineer costs (as of October 2026)

The broadest recent dataset comes from Cargo, a GTM software vendor, which analyzed [509 US GTM engineer postings that disclosed pay](https://www.getcargo.ai/blog/gtm-engineer-salary) on its own job board between April and July 2026. Using the midpoint of each posted range, it found a median of $159,000, with the middle half between $135,000 and $190,000. Founding GTM engineer roles came in around $195,000, usually plus equity. Cargo says almost all of its figures are base or total cash.

Clay's GTM Engineer, Marketing role in New York lists $150,000 to $220,000 base plus equity (posted October 12, 2026), and its Systems and Infrastructure role, open since June 9, 2026, lists $140,000 to $200,000. Vercel's GTM Engineer role lists a San Francisco base range of $180,000 to $295,000 and says total pay may include equity and a bonus (posted October 15, 2026).

An earlier reading came in lower. Bloomberry's sample of postings with public ranges, roughly late 2024 to 2025, had a median of $127,500.

Trust these numbers only so far. The employer ranges skew to well-funded New York and San Francisco companies, we found no posting with pay from a 5 to 50 person company, and Cargo notes only about a quarter of postings state pay at all. Our reading for a small company: plan around Cargo's $135,000 to $190,000 middle band in cash, plus equity, which every Clay and Vercel posting above mentions.

## Why GTM roles are in flux right now

SaaStr's [October 28, 2026 report on ten months of AI in GTM](https://www.saastr.com/10-months-into-ai-for-gtm-that-actually-works-the-cro-matters-more-than-ever-every-other-gtm-role-is-in-flux/) states that "SDR is largely gone as a human job." At Vercel, a lead qualification agent took the function from 10 people to about 1.25 for roughly $5,000 a year in infrastructure and tokens. The 10 moved to other roles, and Vercel's COO stood up a GTM engineering team. In SaaStr's words, "Revenue-affecting agents need an owner in the revenue org."

Databricks shows the other path. Per a [SaaStr write-up of its CRO's interview](https://www.saastr.com/databricks-cro-ron-gabrisko-5-interesting-learnings-on-selling-at-a-200b-ai-company-flat-sdr-headcount-6-10-reply-rates-and-free-seats-that-unlocked-consumption/), Databricks kept its SDR team roughly flat while adding AEs. Loading contacts into what the CRO called "the automated spammer" got 1 to 2% response rates; customized outreach now gets 6 to 10%. SaaStr's line for founders: "Automating SDR work and keeping an SDR team are separate decisions."

Neither report says GTM engineers replace SDRs. Both show that someone has to build and own the automation, whether or not the SDR seat survives. For agents doing SDR work directly, see our guide to [AI employees and what they can actually do](/startup/ai-employees).

## When the hire pays off, and when it does not

This is our judgment from the sources above, not measured outcomes. The hire tends to pay off when:

- You have an ideal customer profile and a message that already gets replies.
- Inbound or outbound volume is high enough that research, enrichment and routing eat someone's week.
- You can see buying signals but acting on them is manual, which ZoomInfo lists among its [hiring signals](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/what-is-gtm-engineering).
- Agents or automations are already running and nobody owns them.

It does not pay off without a working message or channel, because automation scales whatever you already send. Databricks' "automated spammer" era and Sumble founder Anthony Goldbloom's view that the first wave of AI in GTM focused on "sending more" point the same way. Fix the message first, starting with [picking the right SaaS marketing strategies](/marketing/saas-marketing-strategies). It also does not pay off when a founder or one SDR can handle the volume by hand, or when the real pain is forecasting and CRM governance, a RevOps job.

ZoomInfo, which sells such a platform, concedes that some teams do not need a dedicated hire if a platform already handles the infrastructure, and says to hire when engineering capacity is the bottleneck.

## What to have them build first

Our suggested order, drawn from Clay, Sumble and ZoomInfo:

1. Clean the CRM and data foundation: deduplication, enrichment and ownership rules.
2. Account and contact scoring, which Sumble suggests as first builds (account scores refreshed daily).
3. Inbound routing and speed-to-lead alerts.
4. Only then, personalized outbound, with suppression rules from day one.

Each step depends on the one before: scoring dirty records produces confident wrong answers, and outbound on bad scores just reaches the wrong people faster. ZoomInfo's advice applies at every step: "Spend a week validating the signal manually before you spend a sprint automating it."

## How to interview a GTM engineer

Clay publishes a three-step assessment worth borrowing: a business problem investigation (for example, a stalled trial-to-paid rate), a systems sketch, then a mini build. A good submission mentions suppression or multi-channel sequencing; ignoring deliverability or targeting fatigue is a red flag.

Add a live build on your real data. Polished screenshots say little about how someone handles your messy CRM.

Probe prioritization and commercial bias. Clay's manager posting asks for someone who can "confidently say no to projects that don't move the needle," and Clay's hiring guide describes strong candidates asking "Does this workflow actually help someone close a deal?" For the broader process, see our guide to [hiring for startups](/startup/hiring-for-startups).

## The failure mode: spam at scale and bad data

Bad data compounds. In a [SaaStr write-up of a Sumble session](https://www.saastr.com/sumble-why-80-accurate-data-turns-into-33-accurate-data-by-the-time-your-agent-uses-it-and-how-nooks-found-700-accounts-it-didnt-know-about/), Goldbloom walks through five sequential data steps, from company data to job level. At 80% accuracy per step, about 33% survives the chain (0.8 to the fifth power); at 99%, about 95%. That is arithmetic under an assumed 80%, not a measured accuracy for any vendor, but errors do multiply. His example: a new hire named Sam at Anthropic, whom many waterfall enrichment vendors (tools that query several data providers in turn) would map to sam@anthropic.com, an address that almost certainly belongs to someone else. "An agent will send the email."

Volume has rules too. As of October 2026, Google's [Gmail sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126) require anyone sending more than 5,000 messages a day to Gmail accounts to set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC (email authentication records), offer one-click unsubscribe on marketing mail, and keep the spam rate below 0.3%. Automated outbound can cross those lines far faster than a human team. Agents also need watching. SaaStr notes that agents report on their own work, and those reports need checking against the real system.

Before any automated play goes live, require four things: suppression lists, a send-volume ceiling, a human review step on every new play, and one named owner. That last item is the job description in miniature.

## FAQ

### What is a GTM engineer?

A GTM engineer builds the systems a revenue team runs on: data enrichment, account and contact scoring, lead routing, signal alerts and automated outreach. Common tools include Clay, n8n, Zapier, a CRM such as HubSpot or Salesforce, and AI agents.

### How much does a GTM engineer make?

Cargo's analysis of 509 US postings that disclosed pay (April to July 2026) found a median of $159,000, with the middle half between $135,000 and $190,000. As of October 2026, open postings at Clay and Vercel list base pay from $140,000 to $295,000. These are posted ranges, not realized pay.

### What is the difference between a GTM engineer and RevOps?

Vendors call GTM engineers builders of new revenue systems and RevOps the maintainers of process, reporting and forecasts. Bloomberry found 9 of 10 GTM engineer responsibilities also appear in RevOps engineer postings, with RevOps leaning toward forecast accuracy. At a small company it is often one seat, defined by the bottleneck.

### Do I need a GTM engineer?

Not yet if you lack a message that gets replies, since automation scales whatever you send. Consider one when you have a working ideal customer profile and message, enough volume that manual research and routing eats someone's week, and signals or agents nobody owns. If forecasting is the pain, hire RevOps.

### What skills does a GTM engineer need?

Look for CRM fluency, workflow automation and enough code to go past no-code tools. Bloomberry found SQL and Python each in 38% of GTM engineer postings. Clay's October 2026 marketing posting asks for deliverability and database health knowledge plus Python, JavaScript, SQL or TypeScript; Vercel's wants four or more years of full-stack experience.
