# SaaS Valuation Multiples in 2026: What Your Company Is Worth

Public SaaS companies trade at about 4.5x revenue in late November 2026. That is the median of the 64 companies in the [BVP Nasdaq Emerging Cloud Index](https://cloudindex.bvp.com/) on November 26. Private SaaS acquisitions go for less, about 3x to 4x revenue: 3.8x in 2025 and 3.1x in the first quarter of 2026, in [Aventis Advisors' deal data](https://aventis-advisors.com/saas-valuation-multiples/).

Small SaaS businesses are priced on profit instead. They sold for a median of about 4x net income in 2025, according to [Acquire.com's multiples report](https://blog.acquire.com/acquire-com-biannual-acquisition-multiples-report-jan-2026/). For seed and Series A rounds, we found no dataset that gives a revenue multiple at all.

A public multiple is a reference point, not a price for your company. Which number applies depends on whether you are raising or selling, and how big you are.

## SaaS valuation multiples right now

A valuation multiple is a company's value divided by one of its numbers, usually revenue, sometimes profit. A 4.5x revenue multiple on $10 million of revenue means a $45 million valuation.

EV, or enterprise value, is the value of all shares plus debt minus cash. Forward revenue, often written NTM, is the analyst forecast for the next 12 months.

| Market | Multiple | Divides | Source, date |
|---|---|---|---|
| Public SaaS, median of 64 companies | 4.54x | EV / forward revenue | BVP Cloud Index, Nov 26, 2026 |
| Public B2B SaaS, median | 4.97x | Market cap / run-rate revenue | [SaaS Capital Index](https://www.saas-capital.com/the-saas-capital-index/), Oct 31, 2026 |
| Public SaaS growing 25% or more, median of 16 | 9.56x* | EV / forward revenue | BVP company data, Nov 26, 2026 |
| Private SaaS acquisitions, all sizes, median | 3.8x | EV / revenue | Aventis Advisors, 2025 deals |
| Small SaaS sales, mostly under $10M, median | 3.9x | Price / net income | Acquire.com, 2025, 129 deals |

*Our calculation from BVP's per-company data. The SaaS Capital quartiles below are also our calculation, from SaaS Capital's published company data. Run-rate revenue is the latest quarter's revenue times four.

Other sources show other numbers because they track different companies, on different dates, and some divide different things. [Clouded Judgement's November 19 issue](https://cloudedjudgement.substack.com/p/clouded-judgement-111926-security) put its median at 4.3x forecast revenue. BVP's own headline figure of 7.4x is an average, pulled up by a few very expensive stocks, so use medians.

The spread around the median is wide. On October 31, a quarter of the companies in the SaaS Capital Index traded above about 7.6x, and a quarter below about 2.8x. Public multiples also move daily, so check the date on any figure before it goes into a deck.

## What gets a higher multiple

Growth, by a wide margin. In the BVP index, the fastest growers trade at nearly three times the multiple of the slowest.

| Revenue growth | Median EV / forward revenue | Companies |
|---|---|---|
| Under 15% | 3.38x | 22 |
| 15% to 25% | 4.59x | 26 |
| 25% or more | 9.56x | 16 |

Note: BVP Cloud Index companies on November 26, 2026. Our calculation from BVP's per-company data, as are the margin and Rule of 40 figures in this section. It is one day's snapshot, so it shows association, not cause.

Free cash flow margin on its own explained almost none of the differences in multiples. Scores that combine growth with cash flow explained the most.

The Rule of 40 is the best known of those scores: revenue growth plus free cash flow margin should add up to 40 or more. In the same BVP data, companies scoring 40 or above had a median of about 5.1x, against 3.4x for those below. Our [Rule of 40 guide](/startup/rule-of-40) shows how to calculate your score.

Retention is the other lever. In a [November 2025 analysis](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/technology-media-and-telecommunications/our-insights/the-net-revenue-retention-advantage-driving-success-in-b2b-tech) of more than 100 B2B SaaS companies, covering 2019 to 2024, McKinsey found that the quarter with the highest valuations had net revenue retention of 113%. The quarter with the lowest valuations had 98%.

Net revenue retention is the revenue you keep and grow from last year's customers. Our [net revenue retention guide](/startup/net-revenue-retention) covers the formula and benchmarks.

## If you are selling your SaaS company

### Deal size: the $50 million line

Smaller deals get lower multiples. Aventis's data shows a clear break at a $50 million deal value.

| Deal value | Median EV / revenue |
|---|---|
| Under $50M | 3.0x to 3.3x |
| $50M and up | 5.1x to 6.2x |
| All sizes: 2025 / Q1 2026 | 3.8x / 3.1x |

Note: Aventis Advisors, a sell-side M&A adviser, August 2026 report on 543 SaaS deals with a disclosed multiple. Ranges span the medians of its size bands, which pool deals from 2015 to Q1 2026.

Below $50 million, the median barely moves. It is about 3x whether the deal is $4 million or $40 million. So if your company would sell for under $50 million, about 3x revenue is the honest anchor, not the public 4.5x.

You may see 4.8x for bootstrapped and 5.3x for equity-backed private SaaS quoted as current. [SaaS Capital published those predictions in January 2025](https://www.saas-capital.com/blog-posts/private-saas-company-valuations-multiples/), when its public index stood at 7.0x. On October 31, 2026, it stood at about 5x.

### Small SaaS: priced on profit

Business broker FE International's guidance (August 2026) picks the profit measure by size:

- **SDE (seller's discretionary earnings)** is profit plus the owner's own pay and perks. FE uses it below about $5 million in value.
- **EBITDA** is earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, with a manager's salary counted as a cost. FE uses it above about $5 million.
- **Net income** is profit after every cost. It is the base Acquire.com uses in its sales data.

FE switches to revenue multiples when a business reinvests its profit in growth.

On Acquire.com, SaaS businesses sold for a median of 3.9x net income in 2025, across 129 deals. The marketplace says its deals are typically below $10 million in enterprise value.

The profit measure changes the price. In FE's example, $600,000 of SDE becomes about $450,000 of EBITDA once a $150,000 owner salary counts as a cost, so the same multiple gives a price a quarter lower. Ask any buyer which profit they mean before you compare offers.

## If you are raising pre-seed to Series A

We found no dataset that pairs round valuations with company ARR. So there is no measured seed or Series A multiple to give you.

SaaS Capital's public index fell more than 40% in the first half of 2026, then recovered to about 5x by October. Yet [Carta's Q3 2026 report](https://carta.com/data/state-of-private-markets-q3-2026/) found that median valuations at every fundraising stage were significantly higher in 2026 than in 2025. We read that as a sign that round prices did not follow public multiples this year, though the data does not say why.

Without a multiple to lean on, the numbers you can defend are growth, retention and how much cash each new dollar of ARR costs you. That last one is your [burn multiple](/startup/burn-multiple), the efficiency number to have ready when a public multiple tells an investor nothing about your stage.

## Which multiple applies to you

| Your situation | What to use | Rough current figure |
|---|---|---|
| Raising pre-seed to Series A | Growth, retention and burn, not a multiple | No measured multiple |
| Raising Series B or later | Public revenue multiple, minus a private discount | Public median about 4.5x (BVP, Nov 26, 2026) |
| Selling, under about $5M | Profit multiple (SDE or net income) | About 3.9x net income (Acquire.com, 2025) |
| Selling, larger | EBITDA if profitable, revenue multiple if reinvesting in growth | Revenue: about 3x under $50M, 5x to 6x above (Aventis, deals since 2015) |

For Series B and later, start from the public median and expect a discount. SaaS Capital's view (August 2025) is that private multiples are lower than public ones, all else equal, but none of our sources says how much lower at your stage. Your growth and retention decide which side of the median you can argue for.

For a sale, deal size sets the starting point and the buyer sets the profit measure. A profitable business worth about $3 million is unlikely to be priced on a 4.5x public revenue figure, so compare it with profit multiples.

## Before you quote a multiple

Every row above starts from the same inputs: growth, net revenue retention, burn and margin. Our [SaaS metrics guide](/startup/saas-metrics) shows how to calculate each one, so you know which row you belong in before a buyer or investor tells you.

When a multiple goes into a deck or a negotiation, put its source, its date and what it divides next to it. A 4.5x forward revenue figure and a 5x run-rate figure are both current, and the other side will pick whichever suits them.

## FAQ

### What is a good SaaS valuation multiple in 2026?

For a public SaaS company, about 4.5x forecast revenue, the BVP Cloud Index median on November 26, 2026. The SaaS Capital Index put the median at about 5x run-rate revenue on October 31, with the top quarter above about 7.6x. Private acquisitions had a median of 3.8x revenue in 2025 in Aventis's data, and faster growth goes with a higher multiple.

### What will my small SaaS sell for?

If it is small and profitable, expect a buyer to price it on profit. SaaS businesses sold on Acquire.com went for a median of 3.9x net income in 2025, across 129 deals, mostly under $10 million in value. Check which profit a buyer means: SDE adds back the owner's pay, so the same multiple on SDE gives a higher price than on EBITDA.

### Is 10x ARR realistic?

For a public company growing 25% or more, roughly yes: that group's median was about 9.6x forecast revenue in the BVP index on November 26, 2026. For a private sale under $50 million, no. Aventis puts the median for those deals since 2015 at about 3x revenue, and even the top quarter of its deals under $5 million starts at 5.7x.

### Are seed rounds priced on ARR multiples?

Not on any multiple we could measure: we found no dataset that pairs seed or Series A valuations with company ARR. Carta's Q3 2026 report found median valuations at every stage were significantly higher in 2026 than in 2025, a year when public multiples dipped. At that stage, defend growth, retention and burn.
